Prop firm drawdown calculator

Check whether your risk settings can survive a prop-firm challenge. See how many losing trades in a row break the daily and maximum loss limits, and simulate thousands of trading days to estimate how often your settings would breach.

Simulation assumes independent trades with fixed risk, the limits measured from the starting balance of each day and of the account (static drawdown), and no commissions. Real results also depend on correlated trades, slippage and your firm's exact rules. Educational only, not financial advice.

How to use the numbers

  • Losses in a row to hit the daily limit: if this is 3 or less, a single bad morning can end your challenge. Most traders who breach do it on one bad day, not slowly.
  • Breach chance comes from 5,000 simulated challenges with your win rate, R:R and trade frequency. If it is above about 10-15%, reduce risk per trade or the number of trades per day.
  • Overlapping trades count together. Three open trades at 1% each is 3% at risk at once. Treat them as one bigger trade when you read these numbers.

Worked example: a $100,000 challenge

With the default inputs (account $100,000, daily loss limit 5%, maximum loss limit 10%, risk 1% per trade):

  • Risk per trade: 1% of $100,000 = $1,000.
  • Losses in a row to the daily limit: 5% ÷ 1% = 5. To the maximum limit: 10% ÷ 1% = 10.
  • Expected result per trade with a 45% win rate and 1.5 R:R: 0.45 × 1.5 − 0.55 = +0.13R.
  • Breach chance over 30 days at 4 trades a day: about 15%.

Halve the risk to 0.5% per trade and it takes 10 losses in a row to hit the daily limit and 20 to hit the maximum, and the breach chance falls to about 2%. The expected result per trade in R stays the same, but each R is worth half as much, so reaching the profit target takes longer.

Breach chances are this calculator's own simulation averaged over 200,000 runs; a single run on this page can differ by about a percentage point.

Daily vs maximum drawdown

  • Daily loss limit: how much you may lose in one trading day, usually measured from that day's starting balance or equity, and reset at the firm's stated time.
  • Maximum loss limit: how far the account may fall in total. Static limits are measured from the starting balance; trailing limits follow your highest equity, which is harsher once you are in profit.
  • This calculator uses static limits. If your firm uses a trailing limit or measures on equity, check its rulebook and treat these numbers as the best case.

In my own prop-firm simulations (591 simulated challenges), fixing stacked trades took the breach rate from 72% to 0%: risk that adds up across open trades can breach a challenge even when each trade looks small.

Questions

Do open losing trades count toward the daily limit?

At many firms, yes: when the limit is measured on equity, floating losses count. This calculator counts each losing trade's full loss, so treat open trades as already lost when you read it.

When does the daily limit reset?

At the reset time your firm states, often in a specific time zone. Check it in the firm's rules before you trade near the reset.

What breach chance is too high?

If the breach chance is above about 10-15%, reduce risk per trade or the number of trades per day, and run the calculator again.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an educational tool; results depend on your strategy, correlated trades, slippage and your firm's exact rules.
