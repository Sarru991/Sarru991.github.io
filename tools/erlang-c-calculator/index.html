Erlang C calculator for call-centre staffing

Enter your call volume, average handle time and service-level target. The calculator tells you how many agents you need on the phones, how many to schedule after shrinkage, and what service level and wait time to expect.

How to read the result

  • Agents on the phones is the minimum number of people handling calls at the same time to hit your target.
  • Agents to schedule adds shrinkage: breaks, meetings, training and absence. This is the number to put on the roster.
  • Occupancy is how busy agents are. Above about 85-90% for long periods usually leads to burnout and attrition.
  • Average speed of answer (ASA) is the average wait across all calls, including those answered immediately.

The table under the result shows how service level changes with one or two agents more or fewer, which is useful when you have to justify headcount. For a full explanation with a worked example, read Erlang C explained.

What is the Erlang C formula?

Erlang C is the queueing formula contact centres use to work out how many agents they need. It takes the traffic you expect and tells you the chance a caller has to wait, and from that the share of calls answered within your target time. The calculator above uses exactly these steps:

  • Traffic (A, in Erlangs) = calls × average handle time ÷ interval length.
  • Chance a call waits (Pw) = (AN/N! × N/(N−A)) ÷ (Σk=0..N−1 Ak/k! + AN/N! × N/(N−A)), for N agents.
  • Service level = 1 − Pw × e−(N−A) × t / AHT, where t is your answer-within target.
  • Average speed of answer = Pw × AHT ÷ (N − A), and occupancy = A ÷ N.

Questions

What is Erlang C used for?

To find the minimum number of agents that answers a target share of calls within a target time, for one queue and one interval, given the calls you expect and how long they take.

Why add shrinkage?

Erlang C gives the people needed on the phones at the same time. Breaks, meetings, training and absence take paid time away, so you schedule more: on the phones ÷ (1 − shrinkage).

Does Erlang C work for chat and email?

Not directly. It assumes one inbound queue where each agent handles one contact at a time. Chats handled several at once, and email backlogs, need other methods.

Why can Erlang C overstaff?

It assumes nobody hangs up. When many callers abandon, the real queue is shorter than the formula expects, so it can show slightly more agents than you need. Erlang A models abandonment.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The calculator runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent or stored.
