Why your EA works in backtest but fails live: 8 real reasons

Your Expert Advisor shows a smooth equity curve in the MT4 or MT5 strategy tester. Then it goes live and slowly, or quickly, loses. That gap almost always comes from a short list of causes, and most of them can be checked in an afternoon.

1. Look-ahead bias: the strategy peeks at the future

This is the most dangerous one because the backtest looks excellent. It happens when a decision uses information that would not exist yet at that moment. Common examples: using the current candle's close, high or low to decide an entry on that same candle; confirming a pattern with candles that form after the entry; or using a "previous trade result" that had not actually closed yet when the next trade opened.

How to check: make sure every indicator and price value used for a signal comes from closed candles (shift 1 or more). Then run a one-bar delay test: shift every entry one bar later. A real edge weakens a little; a look-ahead bug usually collapses completely. In my own research this single test exposed results that looked like a strong edge but turned out to be entirely a timing bug.

2. Costs are set too low

The tester's default spread is often tighter than what you get live, especially at session opens, during news and at the daily rollover. Commission may be missing, and stop-loss exits get filled worse than the stop price.

How to check: re-run with the average live spread for your symbol and session, add commission per lot, and add a few points of adverse slippage. If profit drops by more than half, the edge is too thin to survive real trading. Short-timeframe scalpers are hit hardest because costs are a big share of each trade.

3. Too few trades

Forty trades with a 70% win rate can easily be luck. With small samples, a few lucky trades decide the whole result.

How to check: aim for at least 200 trades across different market conditions. If the strategy trades rarely, test more years or more symbols with the same rules.

4. Overfitting: settings tuned to the past

If you optimised many parameters until the curve looked perfect, you probably fitted the noise of that period. Signs: lots of inputs, a suspiciously smooth curve, and results that fall apart when a value changes slightly.

How to check: move each parameter up and down by about 10%. Robust strategies give similar results across a range of values. Fragile ones only work at one magic number.

5. No out-of-sample test

Testing on the same data you tuned on tells you very little. How to check: keep the most recent 25-30% of data aside, tune only on the older part, then test once on the hold-out. If you test the hold-out many times and keep tweaking, it stops being out-of-sample.

6. Bad data or bad modelling

"Open prices only" or low-quality tick data can fill orders inside a bar in ways that never happen live. Odd hours like the broker's daily rollover can contain fake spikes that sweep stops in the backtest but could never be traded.

How to check: use "every tick based on real ticks" in MT5, and look at whether a big share of profit comes from one hour of the day. If a strategy wins 90%+ in one specific window, suspect the data before celebrating.

7. The edge is no better than random

Some strategies only make money because the market drifted in one direction during the test. How to check: compare against random entries with the same session, direction, stop and target. If random entries earn about the same, your signal is not adding anything.

8. Live trading is different from the tester

Slower execution, requotes, VPS downtime, overlapping trades and manual interventions all create gaps. Running several timeframes at once can also stack risk on the same move, which matters a lot under prop-firm loss limits.

How to check: forward-test on demo for a few weeks and compare trade by trade with the tester for the same period.

Quick summary

  1. Use closed candles only, and run a one-bar delay test.
  2. Use realistic spread, commission and slippage.
  3. Get 200+ trades.
  4. Keep parameters few and check they are robust.
  5. Hold out data for one honest out-of-sample test.
  6. Use real-tick data and check odd hours.
  7. Compare with random entries.
  8. Forward-test on demo before going live.
